# Memory

**What's here:** what the assistant remembers about your household on its own, what it never
remembers, and how to see, correct or forget an entry.

Memory is separate from the notes you ask it to keep. Notes are things you filed; memory is the
small, curated set of things worth knowing in every conversation — a standing preference, a fact
about someone in the family, a decision and the reason behind it.

Not every deployment has memory switched on. If nothing described here seems to exist, ask whoever
set up your Family Assistant.

## What it remembers on its own

After a conversation goes quiet, the assistant reviews the part of it nobody has looked at yet and
decides whether anything durable was said. Anything it keeps is written as a single line in a memory
note, dated, attributed to whoever said it, and linked to the messages it came from.

It is looking for things that will still be true in a month:

- Standing preferences, and corrections to them — "no, we always take the tram, not the bus".
- Facts about the people in your household, and about the household itself.
- Decisions, and the reasons behind them.
- Routines: how your family usually does a recurring thing.
- The state of something you are working on across several conversations — a trip, a project, a
  purchase — including the constraints, the options you ruled out, and what is still undecided.

Most conversations produce nothing. That is the normal outcome, not a failure.

You can still ask directly: "remember that Sam is allergic to peanuts" writes the entry there and
then, without waiting for a review.

## What it never remembers

- One-off requests, and how a single task turned out.
- When things happen — appointment times belong on the calendar, not in memory.
- Device state, sensor readings, the weather.
- The raw text of search results or documents it looked at.
- Passwords, card numbers, or anything else that is a credential.
- Anything plainly meant for one person: a surprise, a present, something said in confidence.
- Sensitive personal matters — health, money, relationships — that you did not ask it to keep. Ask,
  and it will keep them.

It also never learns from a conversation that took in content from outside your household, such as a
forwarded email or a web page it read, even if you said something worth keeping in the same breath.
Say it again in an ordinary conversation and it will be kept.

## Memory is shared with your household

There is one memory, and it belongs to the household. Anything learned in a conversation that
contributes to memory may surface later in any household member's conversation, whoever is speaking.
Treat it as a shared notebook on the kitchen table, not a private one.

Phone calls and iOS voice sessions never write to memory: the assistant can use what it already
knows there, but nothing said there is ever added.

## Seeing what it knows

Memory lives in ordinary notes, labelled **memory**, on the **Notes** page of the web interface:

- One short **core note** with the household's standing facts, plus an index of the topic notes.
  This one is in the assistant's context in every conversation.
- **Topic notes**, one per person, project or recurring theme, holding the detail. The assistant
  opens these by name when they are relevant.

Each entry shows the date it was said, who said it, and a reference to the messages it came from, so
you can check where anything came from. You can also just ask: "what do you remember about the
Portugal trip?"

## Correcting and forgetting

Correcting is editing. Memory notes are ordinary notes, so you can fix an entry's wording, or delete
a line you do not want, straight from the Notes page. Your edit wins over anything the assistant is
in the middle of deciding.

Or tell it: "that's wrong, we switched to the later train" updates the entry, and "forget that Sam
plays hockey" removes it.

Two things to know about forgetting:

- It removes the entry from memory. It does not remove the conversation it came from — your message
  history is untouched and still searchable.
- It is not permanent in the sense of never coming back. If the same thing is said again later, or
  an old conversation about it is reviewed, the assistant may learn it again. Say so if it does, and
  it will come out again.

If you want the assistant to stop keeping a whole subject, deleting the topic note removes all of it
at once. The core note is the one memory note that cannot be deleted; you can empty it instead.
